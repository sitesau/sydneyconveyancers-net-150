Conveyancing Sydney Explained

When you review Sydney Conveyancers, the most useful signal is the written split between the professional fee and the extra costs that sit outside it. The source page says routine matters are commonly quoted on a fixed-fee basis, while government charges, searches and transfer duty are set out separately before you engage anyone. That distinction matters because two quotes can look similar until one includes only the legal work and the other lists expected outlays line by line. Before you sign, ask for the exact scope of contract review, searches, exchange support, settlement attendance through PEXA, and any added work that could move the matter outside a routine file. Check whether the quote is for a purchase, sale, transfer, off-the-plan matter or commercial property. Confirm that disbursements are itemised in writing rather than rolled into a vague estimate. Ask who will handle questions if the contract reveals an issue before exchange. The source site notes that licensed conveyancers and solicitors quote a fixed professional fee for routine matters, with disbursements extra. This clarity helps you budget accurately for the entire transaction rather than just the legal fees.

Contract Review

Contract review is where unusual clauses, disclosure gaps or settlement dates are identified early. The source page notes that a licensed conveyancer specialises in property transfers, while a solicitor can advise on related legal issues. For a straightforward purchase or sale, the practical comparison is experience with the exact transaction type. First home buyers may need someone who can explain each step to settlement. A family title transfer needs careful document handling. Commercial matters can involve leases, GST and broader due diligence. Off-the-plan matters raise contract timing and disclosure questions that should be checked well before completion. Sellers should check that the contract for sale and prescribed disclosure documents are prepared correctly before the property is marketed. If you are choosing between providers, ask less about titles and more about recent work on the same type of file, who will review the contract, and when complex issues are escalated. Cooling-Off Rules

Not every property file follows the same timetable. The source page specifically highlights first home buyer, off-the-plan, auction and commercial matters, and each one changes what should happen before you commit. For auction purchases, the major issue is timing. The page states there is no cooling-off period at auction in NSW, so the contract needs review before you bid rather than after. For private treaty purchases, the same source says most residential contracts in NSW have a 5 business day cooling-off period from exchange of contracts, ending at 5pm on the fifth business day. It does not apply to property bought at auction, and the vendor may keep the deposit if you withdraw. Off-the-plan buyers should focus on the contract terms and disclosure rules that apply when buying before construction is complete. Sellers should check that the contract for sale and prescribed disclosure documents are prepared correctly before the property is marketed. Commercial buyers and sellers should test whether the adviser is also checking lease terms, GST treatment and settlement requirements, not only transfer paperwork.

Settlement & PEXA

The page sets out a practical file path: review the contract, run the searches, calculate transfer duty and manage settlement through PEXA. That gives you a simple checklist for comparing services, because each stage affects timing and cost visibility. Contract review is where unusual clauses, disclosure gaps or settlement dates are identified early. Searches help confirm what is being transferred and whether further questions are needed. Transfer duty should be calculated before commitment so there is no confusion about funds required. PEXA settlement adds a more transparent digital process for document lodgement and transfer of ownership, which the page describes as faster and clearer. If a quote feels thin, ask whether the written scope covers pre-exchange advice, communication with the other side, preparation for settlement day and post-settlement confirmation. Those details often show the difference between a low headline fee and a well-scoped matter. The source site mentions that settlements are completed online through PEXA, so the process is faster and more transparent.

  1. Identify the file type. State whether the matter is a purchase, sale, transfer, auction, off-the-plan or commercial transaction before comparing quotes.
  2. Request the written fee split. Ask for the fixed professional fee and the separate list of disbursements, government charges and transfer duty assumptions in writing.
  3. Test the pre-exchange scope. Confirm who reviews the contract, runs the searches and manages settlement through PEXA before you sign.
  4. Check the cooling-off period. Verify if the contract has a 5-day cooling-off period or if it is an auction purchase with no cooling-off.

Common questions

How much does conveyancing cost in Sydney? Professional fees for a routine Sydney conveyance are typically a few hundred to around fifteen hundred dollars, and many conveyancers offer a fixed-fee quote. Government charges and disbursements such as title searches and registration are additional and vary by property. Always confirm in writing what is included before you engage anyone.

Do I need a licensed conveyancer or a solicitor in NSW? Either can handle your matter. In NSW, conveyancing can be carried out by a licensed conveyancer, regulated by NSW Fair Trading under the Conveyancers Licensing Act 2003, or by a solicitor. A licensed conveyancer specialises in property transfers, while a solicitor can also advise on related legal issues. Both are available in our network, depending on what your transaction needs.

How long is the cooling-off period in NSW? Most residential contracts bought by private treaty in NSW have a 5 business day cooling-off period under the Conveyancing Act 1919. It runs from exchange of contracts and ends at 5pm on the fifth business day. It does not apply to property bought at auction.
